Box breathing

General wellness information. Not medical advice, and not a substitute for professional care.

Box breathing, also called square breathing, is a counted pattern with four equal sides: breathe in, hold, breathe out, hold. A common count is 4 seconds on each side. People use it when they want a steady rhythm rather than a long, sleepy exhale.

In Nervio this lives in Focus mode, starting at 4-4-4-4. This link opens a 2-minute Focus session. You can also pick Quick (about 30 seconds) or 1 minute on the home screen.

The four sides

Sit comfortably. Soften your jaw and your shoulders. You do not need to fill your lungs all the way.

  1. 1Inhale for 4 seconds.
  2. 2Hold that breath for 4 seconds, without clenching or straining.
  3. 3Exhale for 4 seconds.
  4. 4Pause for 4 seconds before the next inhale. Still no strain.
  5. 5That is one box. Repeat for a few rounds.

If holding your breath feels tight, frightening, or dizzy, stop. Use a pattern with no holds instead. Calm mode is 4 seconds in and 6 out. During panic, prefer the panic exercise, which is shorter and has no holds.

A short session is enough

You do not need a long practice for this to be a real session. One box is 16 seconds, so a 30-second Quick session is one box and a bit of the next. Two minutes is several boxes. Then stop.

After a timed session, Nervio asks whether you feel better, the same, or worse. Worse shortens every part of the next session a little. Better lengthens every part a little, so the four sides stay even. Reset mode is separate and does not change.

What box breathing is not

It is a pacing exercise. It is not treatment for anxiety, trauma, or any other condition, and it will not by itself make you focused or calm. Breathing for focus is the same pattern, used as a one- or two-minute edge before a task. 4-7-8 breathing is a different, slower count for bedtime, with a long exhale.

This is not medical care

Nervio shares general wellness information and breathing exercises. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, and not a substitute for professional care. Stop if you feel pain, faint, or unwell.

If you are in crisis or thinking about hurting yourself, call 988 or text 988 in Canada (9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline), any time, day or night. In the United States, call or text 988 (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). If you are somewhere else, contact your local emergency number or crisis line.

If this could be a medical emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in Canada and the United States).
